## Historical interpretation and limits

Continuing paid maritime organization is not identical to a unified national
court. The Navy Records Society's edition of1642–48 documents describes naval
operations and supply continuing during English civil war, with sailors' pay,
provisions and control of actual ports materially affecting allegiance and
logistics. It does not imply that political crisis is harmless or that fleets
operate without reliable resources. [Documents relating to the Civil War](https://www.navyrecords.org.uk/documents-relating-to-the-civil-war/).

The contemporary account of Lancaster's first East India Company voyage records
merchants pooling stock for ships and trade. It supports distinguishing a
financed delegation/trading venture from automatic territorial conquest, not
adding an unmodeled free private-company account to this simulation.
[Contemporary voyage account, scholarly edition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/voyages-of-sir-james-lancaster-kt-to-the-east-indies/first-voyage-made-to-east-india-by-master-james-lancaster-now-knight-for-the-merchants-of-london-anno-1600/AFE45C8B0A035F41B63A2135D26F07D3).

Neither example calibrates a numerical cohesion percentage or guarantees colonial
success. Ordinary household/community boats and oral wayfinding retain their
separate rules. No continent, culture, religious identity, calendar date, desired
empire size or global colony quota enters this correction.
