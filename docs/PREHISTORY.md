# Prehistory prototype 1

Historical version-1 contract below. New simulations use [Prehistory 2](PREHISTORY_2.md), including savanna-first placement, 100-year steps and revised crossings. Version-1 saves retain these original rules.

Owner-authorized scope: human placement, population expansion, environment-based forager lifestyles, neutral ancestry drift and save/resume. No agriculture, advanced cultures, civilization, ethnic names or playable agents yet. The Simulation layer uses satellite cell colors with a shader opacity control: zero gives white land/light-blue water; one restores satellite colors. Occupied patches are light grey; selected connected same-lifestyle patches are highlighted. This thematic view intentionally retains crisp cell/ecoregion boundaries rather than the smooth natural-surface mesh.

`Prehistory` in Core derives connected patches within existing ecoregions. Disconnected pieces cannot populate one another merely because they share a name. Arrays track regional population, first arrival and eight neutral allele-frequency proxies. These are not real biological races, adaptive traits or cultural ability. Lifestyle derives from local habitat, not ancestry, and connected lifestyle groups are not ethnic identities.

Population capacity uses land area, ecological productivity, growing season, relief, lake coverage and coastal access. Growth is density-limited; migration uses population pressure, traversal time and a bounded fraction of the source population. Synchronous updates prevent same-tick propagation chains. Water routes are bounded graph searches from actual occupied coastal patches: short trips up to 140 km after 15,000 elapsed years; shelf routes up to 600 km with sampled seabed no deeper than 130 m during years 20,000–80,000 of a repeating 100,000-year cycle. These are explicit game approximations, not a dated reconstruction of glacial climate, seafaring technology or sea levels. Existing temperatures, glaciers and sea masks do not change. Coarse meshes can miss narrow straits and shelves; unresolved subcell islands are not separate demographic settlement nodes yet.

Each step represents 250 years; playback controls only schedule fixed steps. Named seed/tick/node streams and ordered routes govern crossings and neutral drift; migrants mix ancestry frequencies. Population counts and marker frequencies are quantized integers. More isolated small populations drift faster. No ancestry value changes carrying capacity, mobility, intelligence or social development.

The generator remains worldgen-3.32. `World.Prehistory` is an optional separate checkpoint with `prehistory-1`, species ID, bound world hash and its own integrity hash. It is excluded from the original generation hash; save readers validate both. Loading old worlds does not alter geography. Habitat graph is reconstructed once and cached by each host. Save/resume and batch size must reproduce identical demographic hashes.

The current Earth placement heuristic favors a suitable African habitat but is not a claim about a single established origin site. Generated worlds choose a seeded warm productive homeland. Setup allows another candidate or moving to the clicked habitable patch. Start locks the origin; playback begins paused. Future sapient packs can replace the explicit species contract; only Homo sapiens is accepted now.

Conceptual references (independent implementation, no source-code translation): [Smithsonian Homo sapiens](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-sapiens), [seasonality and forager density](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01548-3), [Sahul dispersal modeling](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-21551-3). These motivate environmental carrying capacity and geographic migration constraints; the game coefficients are not fitted to those studies. Visual inspiration: [Ollie Bye, The History of the World: Every Year](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-6Wu0Q7x5D0); the user-provided description supplies the requested overlay behavior, not a claim that the video was visually inspected by the implementation agent.
