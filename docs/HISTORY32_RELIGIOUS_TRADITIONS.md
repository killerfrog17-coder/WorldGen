# Structured religious traditions and institutions

17 October 2026. History remains `history-32`; optional realm revision31
enables the nullable `Religion.Traditions` extension. This is not history31,
and does not change world generation. Previous saves retain their literal
rules until **Esc -> Update simulation rules**. Fresh histories enable it.

## Separation of identity, doctrine and authority

Each recorded faith has an inherited, saved doctrinal profile. Cosmology,
afterlife, source of revelation, image/ancestor/offering practices, exclusivity,
orthodoxy, outreach, membership openness, clerical authority, sacred kingship,
asceticism, mutual aid, purity, nonviolence, intermarriage and hospitality are
distinct axes. A ritual calendar records a cycle, principal festival and assembly
rhythm. Calendars are descriptive inputs for later texts, not a new per-day
simulation schedule. These are generated normative teachings, not a census of
what every believer does. No LLM, invented scripture or online service is used.

Lineage is compiled into root and generation IDs, with a recorded parent,
secondary synthesis donor and particular disputed teaching. Descendants inherit
unrevised beliefs rather than rerolling their whole profile. Comparisons expose
separate belief, practice and authority affinities plus the strongest specific
disagreement. Close relatives can disagree sharply over images or sacrifice;
distant accommodating traditions need not be hostile. Differences are not a
universal religious-strength, advancement or moral-worth score.

A religious body is separate from a faith. Two bounded local slots represent
public ritual organization and the existing separately provisioned lay
association. Supported bodies retain their seat, form, autonomy, patron,
contact/loyalty, grievance and review dates. Local forms include shrine councils,
temple colleges, teaching fellowships, monastic orders and central sees. The form
is an organizational description, not a population/staff count. Institutions
start locally. Continuing physically delivered contact and a centralizing
authority tradition can establish shared jurisdiction; decreasing seat IDs keep
the authority graph acyclic. Broken contact, a lost seat or a sustained dispute
can restore local authority without inventing a faith or changing state borders.
Conquest does not directly reassign religious authority.

## Actual simulation effects

- Paid public institutions debit real town provisions; charitable/hospitality
  emphasis changes the provision requirement and permits a small local service
  effect. Starvation guards and existing institutional support decay remain.
- Doctrine affects transmission/adoption, deliberate outreach and the propensity
  to centralize religious authority. Language/physical-route checks remain.
- Court policy reflects institutional government, rulers and doctrinal attitudes.
  Funded exclusion of a conflicting practice generates local grievance. Inclusion
  moderates it; permissive worship allows it to decay. Captured patronage can
  create a distinct authority dispute within the same faith.
- Saved local grievance contributes bounded stability/autonomy pressure. It is
  not a direct independence or territory-transfer action.
- Diplomatic affinity is graded rather than just equal/unequal faith IDs.
  Enforced exclusive positions amplify salient disagreements; nonviolence can
  temper offensive risk. None of this creates a war without the existing
  political, provisioning, frontier and campaign checks or changes battle power.
- A separately provisioned lay organization can articulate a doctrinal branch
  after a sustained actual dispute. The record names the changed issue and
  retains ancestry. Organization, resources, duration and a stochastic hazard
  are required. An institutional split need not become a new denomination.
  Formation does not automatically convert the public community or its ruler.

The Religion map continues to show public traditions. **Religious institutions**
is a separate overlay in View, selecting the authority actually represented,
not an unrelated state or culture. It covers supported local bodies and their
existing market connections, not imagined universal ecclesiastical borders.
The atlas separates beliefs, observances, social teachings, graded relationships,
public worship, household inheritance and institutional jurisdiction. Public
and lay documents have distinct identities even when they share a town.

## Efficiency and compatibility

Ordinary work uses the existing city/culture/contact events and two local slots;
there is no added recurring world/state/culture/ecoregion membership pass.
Institution records use a sparse persistent tree. Pair comparisons use a fixed
number of axes; query time never walks religious ancestry. Profile appends,
explicit upgrades, integrity checks and on-demand atlas/renderer exports may
enumerate actor records. No new daily ritual/calendar queue is introduced.

The optional field is absent from legacy canonical bytes when null. Copies detach
mutable profiles and share immutable tree nodes. Doctrine and body changes are
hashed and serialized. Explicit migration adds profiles prospectively, preserving
names, old events, public/household identity, people, food and political ownership.
It does not claim to reconstruct unrecorded beliefs in old histories.

## Research and limits

The distinction between affiliation, belief and practice is informed by
[Pew's measurement discussion](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2018/07/05/how-does-pew-research-center-measure-the-religious-composition-of-the-u-s-answers-to-frequently-asked-questions/)
and [cross-denomination belief/practice typologies](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2018/08/29/the-religious-typology/).
These sources motivate distinctions, not the model's numbers or a worldwide
theory inferred from US surveys. The model distinguishes exclusivism and
pluralism rather than assuming that different cosmologies must conflict; see
[Stanford's discussion of religious diversity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/religious-pluralism/).
Numeric rates and doctrine distributions are authored game priors, not fitted
historical laws. The model has no complete believer census, individual clerical
careers, fully developed canon/council proceedings, or generated religious text.
Ethnic/language overhauls and a complete replacement of political defection
mechanisms are separate future work, not claimed by this religious revision.

Review evidence is recorded in VALIDATION.md. Focused counterfactuals establish
causal behavior, not a claim of calibrated world-scale religious history.
