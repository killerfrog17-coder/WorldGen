# Contested local sovereignty

History32 optional realm34 builds on the religious institutions, spoken
communities, ethnic identities and local memories of31–33. Generation,
technology costs, observer eras, music and the state palette are unchanged.
Existing saves keep their literal rules until the explicit historical-rules
upgrade. No earlier disputes or conquests are reconstructed during upgrade.

## What changes

Previously a disconnection or autonomy hazard could immediately transfer a
town to a compatible neighbor or found a successor court. Ordinary peaceful
incorporation could similarly label an immediate claim a protection agreement.
These paths now open an inspectable local case first, without changing a border.

Cases distinguish protection requests, organized independence claims and
withdrawal from an inaccessible administration. Each retains the incumbent,
proposed protector if any, start/review/decision dates, claimant support,
offered rights, whether negotiation was paid and its eventual outcome. The
next review is chosen in a named deterministic2–8-year window and processed
by the existing town event, not a new global timer. Town event cadence may
delay it beyond the nominal date. Inclusion and claimant support continue to
evolve during the dispute. Unresolved episodes lapse after roughly60years;
ordinary repeated proposals have a20-year cooldown. These are game priors,
not an empirical universal political timetable.

### Protection is an agreement, not adjacency paint

The receiving court must have a real, supported, living town in the target's
bounded physical contact list, solvent service accounts, adequate legitimacy
and cohesion, and an actual bounded land connection or viable supported sea
administration. It pays100 + one unit per50 town residents for negotiation.
Rejected negotiations can still cost money; a request alone costs nothing.

Local acceptance depends continuously on proposed rights, remembered rights,
actual attributed harm, identity affinity and the existing locally grounded
religious grievance. Similar heritage is not consent, and different heritage
is not an absolute ban. No inhabitants, farms, armies, technical lessons,
religious converts or ethnic replacements are created by agreement.

A neighboring court cannot peacefully steal a functioning reachable incumbent's
town. A genuinely cut-off town can seek protection; an organized local
secession coalition can also negotiate with a nearby recent sibling successor
of the same parent court. Actual reach, receiver financing and local acceptance
remain necessary in that case too.

### Secession, reconciliation and withdrawal differ

A reachable town needs a viable organized coalition with substantial support
and real central crisis or severe autonomy pressure/weak cohesion before it can
found an independent government. Existing local claimant records carry the
organization and leadership. The current hazard can trigger organization in
a supported, established center under those conditions; a crisis alone does
not grant a new state. Proven cut-off, supported institutional centers retain
the possibility of de facto independence without an imaginary field battle.

Reliable supported administration and high inclusion can reconcile a reachable
town. A minor cut-off settlement can instead stop answering to its former
court without automatically becoming a state or being annexed by its neighbor.
An exhausted bounded route search is *unknown*, not proof of isolation.

Successful separation uses the existing population, treasury, levy and service
transactions. It no longer sweeps neighboring towns into the successor by a
province flood fill. Recent sibling governments can receive individual local
agreements instead. A reachable former court with actual funds, army and
manageable exhaustion can initiate a real restoration campaign; ordinary
supplied campaign/siege costs and nuclear restraint still apply. Unsupported
separation does not conjure an independence army or a fake war record.

## Provenance and inspection

Latest town governing transfers distinguish actual military conquest (with its
campaign ID), local state foundation, sponsored settlement foundation,
negotiated protection, secession, loss of administration and abandonment.
Ordinary frontier administration remains separately labeled. A generic
administrative reassignment is honestly labeled rather than retroactively
inventing a battle. Actual conquest and abandonment supersede pending local
petitions. Earlier dated decisions remain in the normal event archive.

City, provincial-center and capital atlas articles expose **Allegiance and
sovereignty** under **Power & relations**, including the latest case and transfer
and links to the real courts involved. Reading is non-mutating. These records
concern local government, not a census of the residents' identities. Existing
imperial patronage/dues, recognized titles, supplied control and battlefield
campaigns retain their distinct accounts and existing inspection sections.
Routine petitions are archived without being added to the global news feed.

`Realms.Sovereignty` is nullable and omitted from older canonical bytes. Its
two persistent sparse trees keep one latest case (12 slots) and one latest
transfer (8 slots) per participating town. Copies share immutable tree pages;
public queries return detached values. Validation checks bounds, dates, parties,
outcomes and required campaign provenance. No regular process enumerates a
world, empire, culture, ecoregion or its cell membership. Existing bounded town
contacts and court route probes are reused. One-time upgrades, explicit integrity
validation and on-demand atlas exports retain their documented exceptions.

## Historical motivation and limits

[The Met's account of Sennacherib and Jerusalem](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/sennacherib-and-jerusalem)
describes succession opportunities, local revolt, restored client rulers and
tribute rather than equating every change of allegiance with annexation.
[Texas State Library's annexation narrative](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/ref/abouttx/annexation/index.html)
distinguishes proposals, ratification and conditions under which independence
or union was considered. These motivate separating a request from an accepted
transfer; they do not supply our coefficients or imply those cases are a
universal pattern. No historical or reference-project code is copied.

This remains coarse, actor-based political modeling: not individual voting,
universal popular consent, detailed legal treaty clauses or tactical local
insurgency. Existing constituent-government treaties are not replaced by this
town-level process. Bounded route probes are approximations. Mechanism and
persistence tests are not proof that all eras or all world's borders are now
historically calibrated. The outstanding dense late-world performance target
remains separate.
