## Shared community capacity

Each local scatter tile samples five locations for accessible grass, herbs,
shrubs, canopy, fruit/seed/nectar opportunities, litter, invertebrate resources,
water and climate productivity. This is derived from terrain/flora data regardless
of vegetation graphics settings. Coarse canopy shelter also follows the existing
stand/gap field. Resources are proxies, not measured food masses or calories.

Every native consumer in the regional species list shares those resources,
including species with no rendered model. Producers and basal resources are not
mistaken for animals. Each food budget is allocated once across compatible
consumers, with body-mass^.75 metabolic demand and local habitat fitness.
Adding another equivalent browser splits the available browse; it does not add
another complete allowance. Evergreen/twig browse remains possible in winter;
warm-season foods and insect activity decline with cold.

Predator support comes from compatible prey in that same community. Existing
Biogeography.CanEat checks preserve prey size, hunting stratum and special diet/
defence restrictions. Each prey biomass pool contributes a small shared predator
allowance, rather than a complete allowance for every predator. Fish-only diets
participate too. This projects potential abundance; kills do not instantly
rebalance the whole region or produce new replacement prey.

Numerical starting budgets are intentionally tunable. Their units are comparative
metabolic demand per square kilometre, not kilograms of actual plant tissue.
The .009 predator/prey biomass reference is shared across competitors here; it
is not a universal measured ratio for every species or ecosystem. Nutritional
quality, seasonality and unrepresented resources limit what can be inferred from
productivity alone.

## Local placement and visible activity

Native species still need suitable immediate habitat: woodland animals require
actual canopy/shrubs; freshwater/coast-dependent lineages need local water/coast
influence; aquatic animals require water. Candidate rejection is stable per
identity, not rerolled every planner refresh. Cold and seasonal-presence rules
remain. A few broad missing nocturnal priors are supplied for civets, badgers,
hedgehogs and porcupines; manual spawning remains available at any hour.

Rendered proposals represent exposed activity, not the entire ecological
population: small ground residents can be concealed/resting, and most birds are
not permanently airborne. This is a deterministic activity sample of the resource
projection, not a hard quota. Existing branch/ground perching remains a prototype;
hidden individuals are not all separately simulated. No animal assets, skins,
animations, bird models or audio were regenerated by this change.

Local standing density uses playable square kilometres. Horizontal world scale
changes which habitats fit in a journey, not an additional 10x/100x density
multiplier. Stochastic group placement naturally differs between scales/locations.

## Encounter time

Only wildlife scheduling uses `effectiveMinutes = clamp(actualMinutes,15,1440)`.
A one-minute solar day therefore has the same encounter pacing as a fifteen-minute
day; a two-day solar day uses the same pacing as a24hour day. The actual sun,
calendar and existing day-length setting are not clamped by this system.

The offscreen travel multiplier is `sqrt(1440/effectiveMinutes)`: about4.9x for
a60minute day, at most9.8x. It blends smoothly from1x beyond the entire visible
range (at least1.5x that range), so visible animals keep their normal speed.
Offscreen decisions/rest periods shorten with this travel clock. Hunger/thirst
schedules use the bounded effective day. Rest/feeding/hunt/migration intent and
terrain/water constraints still apply; no groups are steered toward the player.
Foraging groups choose resource-suitable routes and have larger ranging areas.

This increases opportunities to cross existing animals' routes without
multiplying instantaneous deer/bird density by24. It does not guarantee two deer
per hour, a kill, food sufficiency or a fixed predator encounter interval. Those
need hunting/food playtests. Population density, visibility and travel necessarily
constrain the encounter rates that can be achieved without contrived spawning.

## Persistence and validation

F3+A toggles a north-up3km debug radar. Coloured circles distinguish fleeting,
local, resident and notable tracking tiers. Solid points are loaded actors;
hollow circles are coarse group members not already represented by those actors;
dim groups are outside their active tracking range, and grey points are corpses.
Distance rings use an explicitly labelled expanded near field. The radar reads
existing simulation records without spawning models or querying terrain. It
refreshes at5Hz only while enabled and does not release normal mouse-look.
Manual spawns appear too. Colours/ranges describe simulation tiers, not species.

Population projection version2 clears obsolete procedural group proposals on
upgrade. Wounded animals, corpses, coats, blood, embedded ammunition and command
actors remain saved physical records. Counts are not trimmed to an entity cap.

Targeted checks cover shared budgets, prey dependence, fish diets, habitat
exclusion, seasonal resources, clock endpoints, normal nearby motion and legacy
save versioning. The read-only world audit samples four environments at two world
scales. Its frozen previous-planner source and before/after outputs are under
`artifacts/wildlife-abundance-20261028`; the old planner is not shipped.
The sampled counts include tiny insects and depend on local habitat/RNG; they
are not a universal encounter benchmark. Full breeding, population feedback,
territory ownership and complete navigation remain future work.

One intermediate abundance-only native run failed the existing corpse stretch
guard at2.5296. The final radar build passes at2.2513 with the same2.5 guard;
ragdoll code/skins were not changed. This is variable existing physics, not an
abundance-related ragdoll repair or a universal anatomy/ground-contact guarantee.
Both failed and final evidence remain under `artifacts/overnight-items-20261028`.

## Research basis

- [Carbone and Gittleman2002: carnivore density and prey biomass](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11910114/).
  Supports relating predator abundance to resources/body size, rather than giving
  each predator a fixed independent density.
- [Rowcliffe et al.2008: random encounters and animal density](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2664.2008.01473.x).
  Encounter rate also depends on movement and detection, not density alone.
- [Producer nutritional quality and trophic structure (2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2654170/).
  Productivity alone is insufficient to establish precise consumer biomass.

These motivate the architecture. Game coefficients and the time-compression
curve are authored approximations, not direct results of those papers.


## Firefly and radar follow-up (28 October)

Butterflies/moths and derived fireflies now use the Local tier with a 220 m
tracking/render range. Other tiny insects retain their shorter range. Catalog
metadata and its generation rule agree; original animal bundles are unchanged.
The opt-in radar anchors to the right edge, reserves the actual upper/lower F3
readout rectangles, and places its legend beside the plot when space is tight.

The firefly asset's emission mask was entirely black, so the former shader pulse
produced no emission. A depth-tested additive abdomen sprite supplies the light
instead: species-specific yellow/green colour, soft flash edges and independent
individual phases. Minimum apparent size keeps a light point visible when the
1.8 cm body becomes subpixel. Beyond 40 m the body draw is disabled. Sprites fade
at 220 m and obey the streaming fog; they do not illuminate nearby surfaces or
cast shadows. Instanced submission splits API-sized batches without an entity cap.

Spawning now reads the same existing generated regional Firefly profile displayed
in ecology articles. It requires local vegetated, non-saline, dry-ground habitat,
regional climate suitability and the regional adult display season. Activity
requires warm, moist dusk/night conditions and declines in rain/wind. Noon has
zero flashing and no natural spawn proposals. Existing actors lose their glow
immediately as conditions change and retire through the ordinary planner fade.
These profiles are derived habitat candidates, not saved native taxa or a new
closed-loop larval/adult population simulation. The visual guild represents
flashing night-active fireflies; not all real fireflies flash or are nocturnal.

Evidence for the ecological choices: [Xerces firefly overview](https://xerces.org/endangered-species/fireflies/about),
[NPS firefly display season and temperature](https://www.nps.gov/grsm/learn/nature/fireflies.htm/index.htm),
[University of Illinois firefly natural history](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/good-growing/2022-06-09-sparks-night-fireflies-and-tips-conserving-them).
The numerical 220 m visibility, 40 m body handoff and population density remain
game design parameters rather than empirical biological constants.

Focused checks: 38 regional/profile, activity, pulse and actual Earth-world planner
checks passed, including warm evening candidates beyond the old 35 m boundary,
zero noon proposals and weather/season suppression. Existing 103 abundance and
77 item-use checks also pass. Selected-package native checks pass: glow points at 10/100/200 m, evening
versus noon actor state, and right-edge placement clear of the F3 readouts.
The batch player omits IMGUI repaint, so placement checks use the same production
layout function and actual text metrics rather than an onscreen UI screenshot.


## Shift+F3 target identity

The existing 80 m aimed readout now recognizes live animals, original-skinned
animal/NPC corpses, NPC humans and loose physical items. It reports animal health
or corpse state and item names/materials. Living/dead animal and human targets
use their actual posed surfaces; loose items use rendered bounds. Held/worn
player equipment is excluded. Resident ground, rock bodies and real tree trunks
can occlude targets; an approximate foliage envelope is not treated as an opaque
wall. This is a debug identification query, independent of combat/interaction
permissions, with no new models spawned or remotely generated terrain requested.
The new native checks exercise real deer skin, its ragdoll, a ground rock and
exclusion of that same item after putting it in a hand.

The old obsolete abundance diagnostic package was not removed: automatic approval
review rejected deletion with ?blocked by policy.? No cleanup occurred. The E:
artifact junction and its target must be protected together while selected/running.
