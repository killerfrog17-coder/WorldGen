# History-23: agricultural research without a livelihood-score deadlock

The history-22 pass is preserved in commit `0c4ed2f` and [HISTORY22_REVIEW.md](HISTORY22_REVIEW.md). A subsequent cold-world control exposed a specific failure: after roughly 622,000 elapsed years, this world had computing and digital telecommunications but no crop rotation, synthetic fertilizers or mechanized agriculture. Every culture's farming score remained below 400, even though it had large, supported, actually cultivated rural communities. It was not missing the necessary scientific prerequisites; a broad livelihood proxy was vetoing a local technical activity.

History 23 keeps all history-22 mechanisms and adds a bounded alternative proof of agricultural practice. A culture with at least 10,000 actual catchment rural residents and 5,000 supported urban residents may inspect at most three of its real centers. A qualifying center must have 4,000 rural residents, 1,000 town residents, supported provisioning and nonempty recorded cultivated crops. This supplements the previous threshold rather than removing prerequisites, research-network scale, maturation, crop fit or actual production. The older lower threshold for adopting a known farming practice is preserved; learning does not newly require metropolitan research institutions.

There is no culture-terrain or culture-city census in this check. The three sites are selected by a named deterministic stream. No terrain, climate, biome classification, crop yield, population account, past event or generation hash is rewritten. This is a qualitative game-model correction, not an agronomic calibration.

## Next unresolved political mechanism

The remaining territorial-scale issue is not a reason to multiply every frontier rate. Current peaceful incorporation must pass both a capital-centered reach check and a local service-center check. Actual delegated provincial administration is not represented in the aggregate model, and modern strategic claims in sparsely settled regions can still lag. The legacy dependency subsystem is not a safe shortcut: its broad scans must not be restored to the actor calendar.

A subsequent rules version should maintain explicit local administrative/client relationships and obligation indexes. Each province/client would process its own deliveries, autonomy and response to its patron's strength; patrons would receive cached deltas rather than census their clients or holdings. Direct annexation, client allegiance and civilizational identity need distinct presentation. The Roman transport/local-institution sources in HISTORY22_REVIEW.md motivate that direction, but this pass does not claim to have implemented or validated it. The maps are improved review candidates, not a finished match for all real-world imperial and modern territorial patterns.
