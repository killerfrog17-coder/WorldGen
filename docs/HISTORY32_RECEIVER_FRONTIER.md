# Receiver-frontier learning candidate

Candidate W changes the allocation of existing, finite instruction effort after
real dated contact. It does not increase the research/learning budgets, grant
technology levels or crops, weaken physical contact routing, or bypass material
deployment requirements.

An actual saved East Asian recipient had Writing offer level 7 and all its
immediate prerequisites, but still lacked Writing while having learned
Mechanized Agriculture with four missing prerequisite levels. Agricultural
demand was high because of its large rural workforce. The old learning branch
assigned full demand weights to every delivered offer, even when the receiver
lacked most of the supporting techniques. Independent invention, in contrast,
already checked prerequisite levels.

## Bounded prioritization

The new optional rule weights existing learning projects by the number of
missing immediate prerequisite levels. Integer readiness is
`1000 / (1 + 3 * missing²)` permille, with a 25-permille floor. A genuinely
offered missing prerequisite of another delivered offer receives a twofold
weight. The same conserved learning budget is then distributed by the existing
integer allocation routine. Advanced awareness remains in saved offers and
retains a small positive learning weight; this is not a hard prohibition on
importing unfamiliar practices.

The implementation makes two bounded passes over the fixed technology catalog
and its small direct-prerequisite arrays. It uses stack storage and no terrain,
population, city-membership, contact-ancestry or world census. It never invents
an offer for an absent prerequisite. Existing accumulated learning is not
retroactively redistributed.

`HistoryState.ReceiverFrontier` is a nullable saved rule marker. Fresh history32
initialization enables it; an existing world requires the explicit copy-on-write
`UpgradeReceiverFrontierLearning` operation. A predecessor world stays disabled
even when new cultures arise. The absent marker is omitted from old canonical
hashes, including aggregate hashes; copy, save and validation preserve the
explicit rule. This is distinct from the separate optional port-supplier rule.

## Validation and limits

423 pure checks and fourteen actual integration checks each passed on the saved
V Earth200k and Continents Plus200k controls: absent-offer negatives, bounded
weights, no grants on upgrade, copy-on-write, save/reload, publication
partitioning and actual spent-effort conservation. Unknown marker versions are
rejected. Frozen-V versus current-null twenty-year continuations match exactly:

- Official history31: `f4e260d19883e9dfd894c63c9ff8215646b023fdb25090d3795819e6f48d2fef`.
- V Earth200k: `8f2b9e68b739d83ee660235e21de9771e0a8bf5ec0d8124798158bfd1b809fea`.

A matched 500-year continuation of the earlier actual regional-delivery Earth
save isolates this rule: new port-supplier routing remains absent in both
branches. The named native recipient reaches Writing4 versus3, Iron2 versus0,
and Scholasticism4 versus2; its engines remain1 versus2 because foundational
instruction has higher priority. Its supported urban ledger grows more in the
candidate. Detached regional audits place the gain in actual southern-China
towns: the East Asia box changes from four towns / 92,441 urban residents to six
/ 222,811, including a new 63,449-resident coastal migrant center and a larger
local Hainan/Guangdong network. This remains a very small urban system for a
late modern continent; India still has zero towns in both branches. Actual
screenshots are reviewed separately. This is not a declaration that global
Asian urbanization is fixed or that entire historical chronology is calibrated.

Frozen-V chronology diagnostics also separate first knowledge from first local
deployment. Earth Printing is known at court-year6932 and first installed6959;
Experimental Science at8093/8125. Continents Plus gives Printing6308/6333 and
Experimental Science7095/7119.
Thus the long early-modern critical path is primarily knowledge and prerequisite
levels, not centuries of failed installation. The receiver rule addresses
inefficient adoption; it is not a generic independent-invention speed boost.
