# Tectonic terrain: Earth analogues and acceptance targets

The 3.20 correction narrows and varies continental subduction belts and makes extreme collision relief less automatic. Existing polarity/classification was correct; frequent broad high coastal walls were not. Pair-level coupling/maturity is a synthetic proxy, with subdued margins common and mature strong belts still possible. Foreland and flat-slab relief follow shortening. No broad uplift of continental interiors was needed; source heights already provide an elevated inland baseline. Earth comparisons use coastal-elevation frequency plus visual review, not a global height quota (Earth's ice sheets and Tibetan plateau make that a poor universal mountain-frequency target). Exact coefficients and limitations are in ALGORITHMS.md.

Research/design note, 1 October 2026. This is an independent procedural approximation, not a reconstruction of Earth's geological history. These comparisons guide morphology and provenance; the implementation contract and measured validation remain in ALGORITHMS.md and VALIDATION.md. No reference-generator source or calibrated tables are copied. Continental complexes and world types remain removed; the existing continent-allocation search remains separate from terrain synthesis.

## Mechanisms, not geographic stamps

| Analogue | Geological distinction | Procedural morphology target |
|---|---|---|
| Himalaya / Tibet | Continental collision thickens buoyant crust; a mountain front and broad high plateau are different structures. [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html) | A coherent high front, interior plateau, secondary parallel ranges, passes and a lower foreland; not a thin uniform wall or isolated noise spikes. |
| Andes | Oceanic lithosphere subducts under a continent. [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html) | Offshore trench, overriding-side coastal/interior cordillera, asymmetric cross-section and along-strike variation. Do not put equal mountain belts on both sides. |
| Rockies | The Laramide episode affected continental interior far from the coast, associated with shallow subduction; later uplift and erosion substantially modified it. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/geology-rocky-mountain-national-park) | Some eligible margins create an inland belt with intervening basins. Older/inherited belts can persist without a currently convergent boundary. Label this a historical proxy, not simulated plate history. |
| Aleutians | Oceanic subduction makes a curved arc behind the trench; volcanism diminishes toward the transform-dominated western end. [USGS tectonic summary](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/iscgem851032/region-info) | Open, segmented island tails; convergence controls activity along strike. No automatic necklace around every ocean plate. |
| Japan | An elongated island arc has a back-arc opening history, not merely cones on an arbitrary ocean boundary. [GSJ](https://www.gsj.jp/en/education/geomap-e/geology-e.html), [palaeomagnetic research](https://www.nature.com/articles/317603a0) | Coherent elongated island ridges with straits, trench outside and possible basin behind; distinguish an arc backbone from individual volcano peaks. |
| Indonesia | Sunda subduction transitions toward Banda continental collision; one region combines different regimes. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/seismicity-earth-1900-2012-java-and-vicinity) | Mixed broad islands, small arc islands, bends, gaps, local collision uplifts and ocean basins; never one repeated circular template. |
| Iceland | Ridge spreading overlaps concentrated hotspot volcanism. [USGS ridge description](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html), [USGS hotspots](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/hotspots.html) | A localized broad volcanic island/swell at suitable ridge-hotspot overlap. Ordinary ridges mostly remain submerged; do not turn every ridge into land. |
| Hawaii | Motion carries volcanoes away from a localized source; the chain records an age progression. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/evolution-hawaiian-volcanoes) | Discrete motion-aligned islands grading into older, lower seamounts, with intervening water; no radial spray or continuous uniform causeway. |
| New Zealand | Subduction changes along the boundary; the South Island includes oblique convergence and major horizontal fault motion. [GNS geology](https://www.gns.cri.nz/our-science/land-and-marine-geoscience/geology-of-new-zealand/), [GNS Alpine Fault](https://www.gns.cri.nz/our-science/land-and-marine-geoscience/our-plate-boundary/alpine-fault/) | A narrow mountainous continental sliver can arise from compression plus shear. Pure transform motion alone must not create Himalayan uplift. |
| Cuba | Accreted arc material and arc-continent collision record past tectonics, rather than an everywhere-active volcanic arc. [Primary palaeomagnetic study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040195109000122) | Some elongated subdued/relict arc ridges and islands, with less active volcanism than young arcs; explicitly synthetic inherited provenance. |
| Panama | Arc-continent collision contributed to the eventual isthmus; a bridge is not simply a line between continent centres. [Smithsonian research](https://repository.si.edu/items/84e857a9-178d-4b66-b895-1dcfac9cd825) | Shallow, uplifted arc terrain can coalesce into a narrow connection where continents and an eligible arc are already nearby. Other similar locations remain straits. Do not force arbitrary deep-ocean bridges. |
| Mediterranean | Regional convergence coexists with independently moving blocks, extension, subsiding back-arc basins and uplifted belts. [Primary research](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09064), [rollback and back-arc research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28228-5) | Retain negative oceanic basin relief among curved ranges, peninsulas and islands. Not a uniform mountain ring or a freshwater lake. This does not re-enable the deferred Map2 collision-context or persistent-hydrology systems. |

## Anti-ring rules

The following are generator design inferences from those mechanisms, not universal geological laws:

- Eligibility must use local normal convergence, crust type and subduction polarity. Proximity to any plate edge is insufficient to create an island arc.
- Arc relief belongs on the overriding side, separated from the trench. A broad generic uplift term must not raise the downgoing plate perimeter too.
- Arc strength, maturity, backbone and volcanic peaks should vary coherently along a segment. Terminations, transform transitions and gaps matter more than high-frequency noise sprinkled on a complete ring.
- Do not globally prohibit curved arcs or closed coastlines: atolls and calderas can genuinely be rings at much smaller scales. The reported defect is repeated plate-scale island necklaces.
- Check the final post-erosion land mask as well as pre-erosion feature fields. Smoothing, sea-level crossings and superposed generic uplift can restore a ring even when the arc kernel is correct.

## Proposed acceptance evidence

These are targets, not a declaration that tests have passed. Record actual cases and limitations in VALIDATION.md.

1. Controlled boundaries distinguish ocean-ocean subduction, ocean-continent subduction, continental collision, pure transform, divergence and oblique compression. Check polarity, relative relief, trench/arc ordering and the absence of volcanic-arc uplift on ineligible sides.
2. Review the same seed set before/after in Terrain, Heightmap, plate boundaries and geological provenance. Include small round ocean plates. Measure perimeter-adjacent emergent coverage and inspect suspicious high-coverage rings; a lower global island count alone is not sufficient evidence.
3. A multi-seed corpus demonstrates the distinct morphology families above where their tectonic prerequisites occur. It need not put every Earth analogue into every world, and feature labels alone do not demonstrate visible morphology.
4. Check transects: collision plateau is broader than its mountain front; margin range is asymmetric; eligible inland belt is displaced from the trench; ordinary ridge stays underwater without excess local magmatic support.
5. Hotspot chains have discrete centres and monotone synthetic age progression away from the active source. Ridge overlap changes local swell, not an entire plate's elevation.
6. Verify broad relief after erosion, including open arc terminations, mountain grain, basins and narrow shallow connections. Compare at minimum and representative higher Detail: metre-scale target widths cannot guarantee sub-cell features at 5,000 cells.
7. Preserve deterministic stage hashes, reconstruction parity, cancellation, fixed coarse allocation, connected plates/superplates and no generated persistent hydrology. Version changed terrain and intentionally review golden updates.

## Limits to state explicitly

An instantaneous plate graph plus seeded synthetic inheritance cannot reproduce all the consequences of millions of years of accretion, slab migration, changing sea level and erosion one-to-one. Present-day crust identity is also simplified to whole-plate continent allocation. This work should support recognizable process-consistent feature families, not claim physically complete geology or exact copies of named regions. Broad morphology is testable now; narrow real-world islands and straits remain resolution dependent.
# Version 3.18: physical scale, inherited terrain and water

The accepted 3.17 plate shape algorithm is unchanged. Ordinary hotspot support radii are now 50–130 km, distinct from ridge-hotspot radii of 130–260 km; these are kernel support radii, not exposed island radii. Four to ten progressively older edifices span a requested 500–3,200 km, clipped by owning plate. Broader, low-amplitude swells remain separate from volcanic peaks. This distinguishes individual edifices from the long volcanic trail and swell: USGS describes the [Hawaiian–Emperor chain and Hawaiian Ridge](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/Hawaiian.html); a [54-hotspot synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pepi.2014.07.006) distinguishes edifice and swell scales. Small physical edifices can be unresolved on a coarse mesh; they are not inflated to cell size.

Independent ancient belts have requested lengths 1,000–3,000 km, support radii 140–300 km, ages 180–440 Myr and subdued relief. Dense curved support samples and endpoint taper avoid separate round stamps. Placement is continental and away from active interfaces, with `BoundaryId=-1` and retained owner/age/cell provenance. They mimic inherited terrain, not a simulated geological history. [USGS Appalachian history](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/a-geologic-history-north-central-appalachians-part-1-orogenesis-mesoproterozoic) and [Ural structural research](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005TC001834) motivate long, worn-down belts rather than scattered isolated peaks.

New lake selection is based on depressions and water balance, not a requested lake count. [USGS lake hydrology](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/lakes-and-reservoirs) describes precipitation/inflow against evaporation, seepage and outflow; the implementation simplifies groundwater and seasonal storage. Glacial erosion provenance and snowmelt timing are retained. The [HydroLAKES study](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13603) reports natural lakes of at least 10 hectares covering about 1.8% of Earth's land; that is a sanity comparison, not a quota imposed on generated worlds. Small lakes below a global mesh cell remain unresolved. No observational climate calibration is performed.
