## Ecological origins and physical transmission - history31, 16 October 2026

Fresh histories use31. Independent advanced communities now depend strongly on local growing-season warmth, soil, seasonal harvest/storage, water and plausible native food resources. Initial state institutions additionally depend on actual adopted, locally viable storable crops; a river, long occupation or large population alone cannot substitute. Ever-wet lowland forest incurs a severe clearing/storage/soil penalty. Cool above-freezing days no longer count as equivalent warm productive crop days. Seasonal tropical uplands and genuinely productive irrigated/alluvial sites remain possible. These are continuous environmental factors, not a latitude, continent or Koppen whitelist. Recorded practice, selected crops and deployed crop management/sanitation/fertilizers can ease constraints later.

The requested anti-homeland rule is explicit: after at least100 residents establish a community3500km or more from the human spawn, pristine culture/state hazard within1500km of the spawn is multiplied by0.00001, increasing smoothly to1 at3500km. It does not forbid indigenous development on an isolated homeland before successful distant residence, nor prevent later institutional diffusion back to it. This is an owner-requested game prior, not an inference that Earth's human homeland was intrinsically unsuitable. The hazard ratio is not a guarantee that99% of worlds have distant origins; aggregate opportunities and competing site quality still matter. Rare foundation draws use53-bit named seeded samples so an ordinary24-bit zero bucket cannot overwhelm small penalties.

Cultural expansion, farming migration and actual deliveries now sample an existing fixed terrain raster along their route. Dense humid forest is an intrinsic barrier, including forest-to-forest; arid gaps, relief, frost and latitude adaptation add resistance. Eligible coastal sailing can bypass inland barriers, while crop growth still has to fit. Annual cereals previously exempt from daylength sensitivity now pay a latitude-adaptation cost; selected cultivars broaden their range. Road/transport and later communications ease exchange. Remembered peers no longer become perpetually fresh teachers without another actual delivery; technical offers retain delivery dates rather than reading new discoveries through an obsolete contact. Primitive human dispersal is unchanged.

Ordinary events use fixed8-sample route queries, bounded crop/contact lists and bounded FIFO derived caches. There is no new recurring world/empire/culture/ecoregion census or event schedule. `OriginReach` is nullable, saved and hashed only when present; older saves keep their original behavior/hash until explicit **Esc -> Update simulation rules**. Upgrades preserve already-established actors, names and recorded history, not retroactively move civilizations. World generation remains3.32. The project shortcut targets the verified31 player after packaging;30 remains available.

Mechanism references, not fitted numerical coefficients: [Mayshar et al., appropriable cereal surplus and state origins](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086%2F718372) motivates distinguishing storable/collectable staples from generic productivity; it is one explanatory hypothesis, not a universal cause. [Crop geographic adaptation review](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-060223-030954) supports explicit photoperiod/growing-season adaptation. [Lowland Maya lidar evidence](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10097677) documents extensive tropical agriculture, cities and infrastructure: tropical states must remain possible, especially with seasonal environments and investment. All numerical penalties here are procedural priors, not measured historical likelihoods.

## Procedural language foundation - history30, 15 October 2026

Fresh histories now use30, retaining29's research costs and all demographic, economic, political and geographical rules. Language profiles are an optional saved extension, aligned with cultural actors; older saves keep their original names, hashes and continuation until explicitly upgraded. Upgrading creates profiles for future naming without rewriting established proper names or archived events. The initial profiles of an upgraded old world are procedural approximations, not a reconstruction of its unrecorded linguistic past.

`LanguageGenerator` owns the shared `language-1` sound table, weighted onset/vowel inventories, syllable and coda/cluster preferences, stress, consistent Romanization, compound order, a small sentence-order scaffold and a prospective script category. Actual writing is displayed only for societies knowing writing; no invented glyph fonts, text-to-speech, full grammar, English translation or environmental phonology stereotypes are implemented. The sound inventories are authored procedural priors, not copies of Earth languages. New roots are keyed by family seed and a stable, sense-specific concept ID; a river bank and a financial bank need different IDs. A root has the same form alone and inside compounds. Names reuse descriptive, personal and geographical elements. State names can share their capital's identifying root. Existing settlement names remain historical names when the spoken language changes.

Migration/colonial descendants inherit the actual source's profile, common root seed and precomposed sound map. Local traditions retain the existing model's language-lineage decision; cultural exchange alone does not universally imply common language ancestry. Small regular changes are composed into the bounded map, including changes to borrowed roots. Cultural transformation may evolve pronunciation without inventing an entirely unrelated name. Existing contact events can introduce at most12 dated, donor-attributed, phoneme-adapted loan roots in selected technical/institutional domains when the source knows the corresponding practice. This is a small contact mechanism, not a full historical lexicon.

Culture profiles expose sounds, sample roots with IPA-like pronunciation guides, compound/sentence order, writing status and recorded borrowing. `Name` supports peoples, towns, states, people, houses, regions, continents, oceans, seas, rivers, lakes and religions. `ForeignName` provides deterministic adapted names or translated compounds for a future language-lens viewer; no map-wide lens UI or authoritative named-ocean/sea partition is added yet. Callers must retain a place's source profile/epoch and semantic identity if they need exact historical exonyms after language changes. Names already stored on actors are never recomputed merely by viewing them.

Word/name queries use fixed-size inventories, at most12 loan comparisons and one precomposed sound map, never an ancestry walk, vocabulary search, cell census or per-place simulation event. Cost is O(concept-ID length + emitted sounds), with concept IDs capped128 characters. Actor snapshot publication still copies saved language profiles along with other actor records; this is not a claim that unlimited snapshot publication is constant-time. Larger grammar, writing glyphs, linguistic geography, endonym archives and speech remain extensible future work.

## Connected development and technological reach — 15 October 2026

New histories use19. Existing18 histories retain their recorded algorithms until **Esc → Update simulation rules** (the action is now shared by both hosts). Updating preserves people, actors, dates and the archive; it cannot repair an implausible recorded past. Generation, accepted climate/hydrology/ecology and their hashes are unchanged. Old18 continuation is checked against the previous executable, not merely against itself.

The underlying failures were fixed frontiers despite improving transport, city-only overseas opportunities, one sampled learning contact, birthplace-only cultivation, excessive compounding of institutional readiness, a cold-winter storage advantage without an annual growing-season cost, and treating adoption like independent invention. Winter amplitude is no longer a substitute for reliable warm-season production. Seasonal warm environments and floodplain acreage can support surplus without promoting evergreen jungle or desert cells wholesale. Crops can be studied at bounded nearby inhabited sites; the native-progenitor and cultivar-transfer checks remain.

Knowledge follows regional relays, neighboring communities, city contacts and sea crossings. A recipient considers its bounded contact set rather than losing an available teacher to an unrelated random sample. Known practices can be adopted by smaller supported institutions; independent research still requires sustained surplus, towns and suitable institutions. A bounded six-partner network can support research without every contributing city belonging to one culture. This is a network-support proxy, not a reconstruction of universities, laboratories or industrial supply chains.

Transport increases actual cultural/state frontier work, not just a nominal radius. State claims pay terrain-sensitive incorporation costs and retain service, fiscal, cohesion and succession constraints. Premodern writing/roads support larger funded administrative networks. War opportunities consider relative capability, resources, relations and ambition; campaign throughput responds to transport and forces. Provincial independence retains a bounded neighboring hinterland. Printing enables, but does not guarantee, representative successors. There is no prescribed modern country count or identical expansion timeline for every planet.

Coastal communities can undertake voyages before an urban port happens to form. A deterministic static shore index builds at most16 physically checked destinations per region using bounded directional probes. Recurring voyages examine at most4; vessel capability limits range, population/provisions constrain departures, and suitable crops must actually travel. Indigenous inland societies still learn along inhabited routes, not from a global technology clock. Small sub-cell island patches remain possible stepping stones. The coarse ocean mask can miss narrow straits and indirect passages; naval logistics are approximated, not simulated vessels.

All ordinary work remains actor-local. Frontier actions cap at64, campaigns at12, and a secession explores a bounded128-entry neighborhood. No recurring event enumerates an empire's, culture's or ecoregion's terrain membership. Initialization, diagnostic maps, explicit integrity validation and rendering may traverse terrain. Coefficients remain transparent gameplay priors; these changes do not establish scientific validation or claim that modern borders, demography or geopolitical diversity are finished.

## Settlement life cycles and connected development — 14 October 2026

History18 keeps history17's actor calendar and population accounts, but replaces several defective behavioral shortcuts. New worlds use18; older worlds require **Esc → Update simulation rules**. Loading does not rewrite a save. History17's null optional extension keeps its original integrity encoding and replay. An upgrade cannot undo centuries of already-recorded implausible history; use a new history to assess the new origins and settlement pattern.

* Town foundations require real households, an uncommitted share of a region's land, suitable local crops, and physical separation from other centers. Secondary foundations transfer households from an existing center's rural account. Fixed spatial buckets support bounded neighborhood queries; no world, state, culture or region membership census occurs inside an event. Regional settlement totals are maintained by deltas, not estimated by rescanning towns or cells.
* Urban support depends on the supply account, local production and technology-dependent urbanization. A village does not receive a free identical food source in every neighboring cell. Catchments compete for available regional land. Crops, local husbandry, soil, evergreen clearing, seasonality and floodplain acreage remain relevant. Long-practiced marginal cultivation has a rare path forward; tropical settlement is not categorically prohibited.
* Sustained provisioning disruption, conflict, decline and emigration can end a settlement. Surviving households return to the countryside; the site's date and narrative remain archived. Its live index, city membership and annual event retire. Later occupation is a new dated settlement, not an immortal city. Local hazards are uncalibrated priors, not a reconstructed river-course or urban-life-span model.
* Regional exchange can relay through inhabited decentralized communities. Both unallocated rural people and cached city/catchment populations count as habitation. Up to12 bounded contact opportunities avoid penalizing a highly connected region with a single once-per-century random neighbor. Transfer follows actual neighboring regional routes, with terrain, environmental contrast, distance and maritime capability affecting contact. Neither an uncolored region nor an empty **unallocated** population store is an automatic knowledge barrier.
* Contacts can transmit cultivation methods as well as suitable crops. Local progenitors and crop transfer fit still matter: knowing that cultivation exists does not make a foreign food plant grow everywhere. Early forager maritime traditions remain possible without first requiring a state. Voyages sample coast-bearing regions, can connect ports or establish supported colonies, and do not mistake a full forager carrying capacity for a full agricultural one.
* State origins require supported concentrations and storable surplus. Pristine origins remain rare; neighboring examples and a culture's own dated institutional lineage support derived courts. A later court of the same civilization is not mislabeled as a new pristine origin. Advanced research requires supported centers and institutions, while actual contact speeds adoption. Cultivation is not invented from a nonzero minimum farming score.
* Subject peoples can participate in state institutions and learn from their governing culture without first needing an independent state; annexation does not overwrite their local culture. Existing town communities contribute practical surplus and maritime opportunities, rather than the birthplace habitat determining those opportunities forever.
* Elite pressure responds to finances, administration, conquest and warfare rather than increasing inevitably with age. A contested succession can replace a government while retaining its territory, towns, culture and population. Persistent successor links transfer accounts without visiting holdings. Conquest, secession, loss of centers and genuine court dissolution remain different events; civilizations are not erased whenever a government ends.

The unchanged limitations of the aggregate architecture still apply: administration approximates service reach, local land contacts are not fine-scale road-network solves, ocean navigation uses a coarse physical mask, rural allocation is aggregated, and capability/economic rates are gameplay priors. The changes are not a claim of scientific or archaeological validation. Earth is a terrain/environment control, not a scripted geography of civilization origins.

These support qualitative mechanisms, not the numerical probabilities or a ban on rainforest civilization.

## Actor and population-account architecture — 14 October 2026

History17 introduced the replacement of the recurring terrain-cell simulation with an actor calendar. It is an output-changing model upgrade, **not** an output-equivalent optimization of history16. Generated terrain, climate, ecology, and their stage hashes are unchanged. This paragraph describes the history17 transition; current new histories use18. Existing saves retain their recorded rules until **Esc → Update simulation rules**; updating does not overwrite the original save. Migration preserves the complete dated archive, capabilities, city identities, and global population. Former fine administrative projects/offices are retained in an immutable archival record; future budgets use the new accounts. Initial redistribution into catchments can change a country's estimated population without changing the world total.

Ordinary events never enumerate the world's cells, a polity's cells, a culture's cells, an ecoregion's cells, or a city's supply cells. They do not call the old terrain routing, market, district, religious, or global annual/cultural passes. `HistoryAggregateCalendar.cs` is the complete active dispatch boundary.

| Actor | Typical interval | Recurring work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inhabitable region | 100 years | Scalar remote/forager demography; one sampled dispersal route; local emergence opportunity |
| City | 1 year | Urban/suburban/rural catchment accounts, harvest/reserves, one trading partner, disease, local institutional opportunity |
| Culture | 40 years | One frontier opportunity/contact; six crop systems; bounded capabilities, husbandry and religious exchange; one local settlement opportunity |
| State | 5 years | Aggregate census/budget, administrative reach, leadership, one provincial sample, local diplomacy/frontier opportunity |
| Campaign | 1 year | Bounded frontier/linked-city battle candidates, casualties, conquest and peace |
| Remote community after a court falls | 100 years | Continued aggregate demography, without teleporting its residents or deleting them with their state |

Intervals have deterministic ±30% windows, with staggered initial phases. Actors retain integer-second dates; no global annual turn or synchronized city-founding cohort is introduced. Research, crop selection/transfer, ecological barriers, medical effects, leadership, secession, religion, sea exchange and settlement continue at this coarser computational level. They are game-model approximations, not scientifically validated reconstructions or an exact continuation of16's numerical outcomes.

Cities maintain urban residents, suburban households and surrounding rural residents separately. Countries receive deltas when their cities change, plus an independently maintained remote population. Global population is a stored conserved total rather than a cell census. Local inspection downscales these accounts approximately; cultural remote composition and land administration are also approximations, not a household-level census. An ended government's remote community retains its spatial cohort and its own lower-frequency event until reclaimed. Political collapse does not require visiting former territory. Local culture and ruling state remain separate map layers.

Routine appointments, contacts, trade agreements, ordinary battles and administrative incorporation remain recorded but do not crowd out major news. Political significance uses current/recorded peak population, and old headlines expire. Campaign conclusions carry meaningful settlement outcomes instead of being replaced by a generic peace message. Population/land-area sorting displays the exact ranking value, with area units; the abstract default score stays hidden. Inspection and campaign export expose leadership, institutional traditions, notable/current biographies and dated campaign records, alongside the existing city and religion directories. Longer news text is scrollable in the native popup.

Remaining limits: first-homeland dominance, imperfect simulated Earth climate/hydrology, coarse town locations, resolution-sensitive institution counts, abstract fiscal/levy economics, simplified sovereignty and sparse prehistoric sea routes. Tributes are not recurring dependencies; there are no individually simulated generals, tactical armies, complete dynastic families, local streets or commodity prices. These changes aim at better procedural causal variety, **not an archaeological validation or deterministic reconstruction of Earth**. No continental, ethnic or ancestry development coefficients exist.

The repeatable audit selector is **10** for history-5, with 9/8/7/6 retaining histories 4/3/2/1. Reviews now include `state-foundations.csv`, institutional traditions, political lifetimes, leadership/campaign summaries and one-time settlement outcomes. Compare exact saved geography and founding locations before changing more coefficients. ## Frontier demographic correction — 4 October, final session

New histories use `history-4`; history-1/2/3 saves retain exact continuation. This is a narrow demographic/emergence correction over history-3, not new geography, a replacement climate model or a continent-specific development bonus.

Low-density rural populations can expand into available habitat faster than the very low long-run net growth previously imposed everywhere. For population `N` and current resource capacity `K`, the century growth coefficient is `0.048 + 0.16 * clamp(1 - N / max(1,K), 0, 1)^2`, quantized to millionths. The existing logistic `(1 - N/K)` term still applies; at or above capacity the original coefficient remains. Capacity, glacial/resource fluctuations, habitat familiarity, route probabilities, founding-group transfers and knowledge constraints are unchanged. The coefficient is a game prior, not a fitted archaeological estimate. [Forager limiting-factor research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32750-x) and [stochastic Sahul peopling models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-21551-3) motivate separating local expansion potential from long-run environmental limits; neither supplies these numerical values.

A patch containing many resident communities no longer receives exactly the same single institutional-emergence opportunity as a tiny refuge: eligible regional emergence uses a sublinear exposure factor `clamp(sqrt(population / 2000), 1, 4)`, also quantized to millionths. All prior knowledge, density, resource-storage, pressure and local-site requirements remain. This does not directly create cities, grant crops/technology or paint the entire ecoregion as a state. It reduces one patch-size bias but does not make the simulation resolution-independent.

Peaceful foreign market catchments remain accessible without a formal treaty, at a 1.35 transport-cost multiplier; a treaty removes this friction and war blocks access. This corrects an artificial food-supply cutoff when an existing town acquires political institutions. Disjoint supply allocation, physical reach and local food yields remain unchanged. The multiplier is a game prior: [Assyrian merchant records](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325851) illustrate trading networks across political communities, not universal free trade or this numeric penalty. The model still lacks explicit tariffs, commodity prices and embargo policy.

`--history-town-trace <world> <town-id> <years> <csv>` follows population, provisioning, reserves and health annually for 1–1000 years without overwriting the source save. A controlled replay of the flagged 200k-world town distinguishes temporary shortage from the previous automatic-border cutoff; the corrected market restores access at the next decadal market calculation.

The read-only review also reports named-site population share (including villages, not a historical urbanization rate), political lifetimes and short-lived states, declarations per recorded state-years, and surviving dominant religious traditions. These are calibration diagnostics, not universal targets that the model must match.

Earth controls now populate East Asia much earlier and support several local cultures/towns there by the first medieval endpoint. The first homeland still dominates too strongly; India remains weak, and complete prehistoric settlement of the Americas is not established. The same generated-continents, archipelago, cold and desert controls remain necessary. Do not equate modeled settlement institutions with the existence or worth of human culture.

The audit selector is now `9` for history-4; `8` retains history-3, `7` history-2 and `6` history-1. Existing worlds are never silently migrated. New versions retain the history-3 compact archive layout, with the history version itself still included in the canonical checkpoint hash.

## Era-aware iteration — 4 October 2026

The preceding release introduced `history-3`; history-1 and history-2 retain their exact continuation and original hash formats. Geography remains worldgen-3.32. The model separates crop domestication, supported specialist practices and state formation; none follows automatically from elapsed years or continental identity.

Six replaceable crop-system archetypes (small grains, warm-season cereals, wetland grain, roots/tubers, orchard/nut crops and pulses) have different temperature, growing-season, moisture, salinity, relief and storage traits. Seeded ecological-province availability constrains independent domestication. Cultivation accumulates practice; compatible crops can arrive through active contacts. Adoption year and source culture are saved, validated, hashed and displayed. Neighboring residents can adopt an established crop system and form a distinct farming community without independently reinventing cultivation or automatically founding a city/state. These are **not literal species-level native Earth crop inventories**; domestic-animal breeding remains future work.

Writing, navigation and other specialist practices require a sustained local economic/institutional base and mature prerequisites. Small supported nonfarming communities can still work copper; farming is not a universal prerequisite for complexity. Research sources include [the Met on writing and Uruk administration](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/the-origins-of-writing), [NPS on native copper use](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/why-copper.htm), [protracted plant domestication](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1308937110), and [crop adaptation and geographic spread](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-arplant-060223-030954). Numerical thresholds are game priors, not estimates fitted from those sources.

Rural inhabitants are apportioned by quantized local habitat capacity rather than equally across a connected ecoregion patch. Ordered prefix allocation preserves the exact patch population, with zero allocation to zero-weight cells. This is a coarse distribution, not household placement or a fully dynamic farm-location model. Candidate-site diagnostics use the actual model's density/storage/water/knowledge quantities. Sailing can cross submerged shelf routes as well as deep-water routes; route selection now considers acquired navigation rather than selecting a closed landbridge and ignoring usable shipping.

History-3 uses a compact, explicit event hash layout: all event fields and archive length are rehashed on every verification, but repeated field/type labels are omitted. No archive is truncated or trusted without verification. Earlier formats remain byte-identical. This reduces hashing overhead, not the linear memory/load cost of an indefinitely growing chronicle. Browser loads now forward reading/integrity progress; technology inspection includes adoption dates and crop introductions name their source culture.

Successful founding groups carry their departure community's accumulated practical knowledge into previously empty patches, including across sea routes. This uses the prior demographic checkpoint, not within-step cascading diffusion. It does not grant domestic crops, historical technologies or institutions; those still need local support and transmission. The earlier forgetting-on-arrival behavior is retained only for legacy histories.

Campaign chronicle exports now include all settlement records (including declined sites), coarse coordinates and habitat, current political/cultural affiliation, food/health/stability, linked local milestones, and a religious-tradition directory with parent traditions and holy places. States name their parent state and current or last-recorded ruler/capital. These are coarse narrative anchors, not street plans, a complete genealogy or a census of minority faiths. The full linked event archive is retained after the directories.

The first-origin advantage remains substantial. Earth uses simulated, imperfect climate/drainage rather than observed historical conditions; its Indian-region storage opportunities and Asian settlement timing remain weaknesses. Do not interpret the era maps as reproductions of Earth history, or infer innate differences between peoples. No race, ancestry, country or continent development coefficients exist.

## Earlier iteration: history-2

The earlier release introduced `history-2`; both that version and history-1 retain their original rules and exact continuation. The prehistory envelope stays `prehistory-6`. The following describes the retained history-2 base; the history-3 section above supersedes its crop, specialist-practice and rural-allocation rules.

Agricultural productivity, locally concentrated storage opportunities, and political centralization are separate. Seasonal grain-like crops, wetter mixed gardens/tree crops, and aquatic resources offer different opportunities; rainforest is not an absolute exclusion. A river can offer an excellent settlement site inside a dry cell without irrigating that entire cell. River-supported forager capacity and settlement-site density use a bounded corridor proxy. Cultivation spreads more readily between compatible climates and current contacts, rather than every contact ever recorded. Wild reindeer presence no longer automatically implies domesticated herding.

Urban provisioning uses disjoint terrain-costed catchments with physical reach (65 km baseline, extended by roads, accounting and rivers), real land area, partial edge-cell overlap and a physical area cap. This removes the former fixed 8,000 km² allowance per cell. Small-island nodes use the largest island's land area, never the ocean cell's area. These are coarse food-export proxies, not mapped farms or a calorie-balanced agricultural economy. A stable nearby state can sponsor a new town; not every successful settlement must found an independent polity. Defeated capitals relocate immediately when other cities survive, and levy casualties come from the state's own towns proportionally.

Cultural sea contacts use occupied coastal endpoints and acquired sailing/navigation. Contact IDs remain for lore, but current geographical contacts govern diffusion. Trade can transmit religious practice independently of conquest. Alliances can lapse under food stress or succession rather than becoming permanent peace locks. These are seeded game priors, not empirically estimated universal rates.

[Amazonian polyculture](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.QUASCIREV.2020.106582) cautions against equating farming solely with cereals. [Sahul voyaging research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-42946-9) motivates separating purposeful sea travel and viable founding groups from shelf crossings. No continent, race or ancestry coefficients were introduced.

UI: opacity shortcuts are **25 percentage points**, with Shift selecting societies. Auto restores phase-dependent year steps after a manual override. Categorical territory/faith colors no longer interpolate across cell boundaries over smooth terrain; base terrain colors still interpolate. Existing cached mesh variants support immediate layer and opacity changes. Island polity colors apply to island geometry, not surrounding ocean, and settlement marks use the largest island's actual center. Native inspection and legend text are cached by checkpoint/selection. Loading reports reading versus integrity checking. Canonical integer-array hashing batches identical bytes, preserving existing integrity hashes.

Known limits: simulated Earth climate/drainage is not observed historical geography; plant domestication uses broad resource proxies rather than explicit native crop cultivars, and settlement counts remain resolution-sensitive because cities occupy coarse cells. The first populated region retains a substantial head start. Sea routes are sparse coarse opportunities, not sailing expeditions with prevailing winds. Long-lived archives increase checkpoint/save costs. A plausible map is not a validated reconstruction of the timing, population or political geography of real Earth.

## Repeatable review loop

`powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/history-check.ps1 -Screenshots` creates a fresh audit folder and generates 20k-cell Earth / Continents Plus seed 17 / Archipelago seed 42. Each run stops **750 years after one culture acquires all three medieval capstones** (steel, navigation, scholastic learning); these avoid requiring context-specific irrigation everywhere. `-AfterMaxTech` accepts 500–1000. `-Years` is a safety ceiling (default 200,000), not an instruction to continue long after the technology ceiling. Failure to attain the capstones is reported, not disguised as a complete progression. `-Detail 200000` exercises larger maps.

Outputs include `eras.csv`, `audit-summary.txt`, `timeline.csv`, dated state/religion/technology SVGs, a four-stage `era-storyboard.png`, site/population/candidate/sea-route CSVs, the full chronicle and a resumable world. Diagnostic screenshot years are the first sampled checkpoint at or after each milestone; event dates themselves remain exact. Playwright/Edge renders screenshots. `--history-audit <world> <new-output-folder> <safety-year> 8 --after-max-tech 750 --continue-history` resumes rather than resets a save. Omitting `--after-max-tech` retains a fixed-endpoint stress test; selectors 6/7 retain history-1/2 comparison runs. Snapshotting an already-completed save still reports its existing milestones. `--origin-patch N` selects a controlled alternative homeland for a fresh run, never for resumed history; it changes placement, not geographic development coefficients.

A tested but rejected pass multiplied independent cultivation practice by a strong glacial-intensity penalty. Although it delayed farming, it left metallurgy preceding cultivation in the sampled histories and did not remedy first-origin dominance. Intensity alone is not climate variability, and the historical climate-stability hypothesis is not a universal mandatory-agriculture rule. That modifier is not in the released model. Alternative East Asian and Indian origin controls show that arrival timing explains part, but not all, of the regional imbalance; crop/storage habitat proxies also matter.

Review first-city locations against water, productive land and terrain; compare regional institutional coverage rather than counting all societies as states; investigate unoccupied sea gaps with distance, source navigation, founding population and destination capacity; inspect state/religion maps on the same seed and year. Keep rejected passes and notes under ignored `artifacts`, not as new golden expectations. Regression commands are `--history-only`, `--history-presentation-only`, `--coastal-only` and `--hash-batches-only`. Native `--worldgen-history-resume` checks the same 37-year continuation, optionally with `--worldgen-history-benchmark` for annual playback cost.

Each audit also emits `review.txt` and `settlement-review.csv`: habitat counts, dry/short-season/food-stress flags, technology distributions and occupied-to-empty sea opportunities. Flags invite investigation, not automatic rejection or geography-specific tuning. `--history-review-only <saved-world> <output-folder>` generates these observations without advancing history. Sea rows can include distinct shelf-depth alternatives for the same patch pair; navigation is a patch-level summary, not evidence of a fleet at every coastal endpoint.

This owner-authorized extension supersedes the earlier history, war, religion and technology deferrals. Geography still comes exclusively from WorldPipeline and remains immutable. This is an **end-to-end procedural historical model**, not an archaeological reconstruction or a validated prediction of real societies. It supports campaign-world histories; it does not simulate individual playable people, local street plans, tactical combat or a complete economic system.

## Research and interpretation

- [The Met: Uruk](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/essays/uruk-the-first-city) describes urban concentration, specialist production and institutions. The model therefore creates settlements before states, and separates an urban center's reach from cultural identity. A cultural promotion never paints all its cells as urban civilization.
- [Fuller et al.: multiple domestication pathways](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1308937110) cautions against one inevitable agricultural transition. Cultivation here needs opportunity, pressure and accumulated practice or contact. Habitat, growing season, fertility, salinity and relief constrain attainable cultivation; time alone does not unlock a state.
- [NPS: Poverty Point](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/monumental-earthworks-of-poverty-point-world-heritage-site.htm) documents monumental hunter-gatherer societies. Concentrated fishing and floodplain resources offer a non-farming path to storage, sedentism and regional institutions. Not every complex society is an urban state.
- [The Met: Great Zimbabwe](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/great-zimbabwe) and [Teotihuacan](https://www.metmuseum.org/TOAH/hd/teot/hd_teot.htm) are counterexamples to treating Africa or the Americas as broadly incapable of complex societies. There are **no continent, race or ancestry multipliers** for political development. Knowledge means transmitted practices, not innate intelligence.
- [Turchin et al.: war, space and complex societies](https://doi.org/10.1073/PNAS.1308825110) motivates testing spatial warfare and institutions as interacting mechanisms, not copying its model or treating war as the sole origin of states.
- [Cambridge: Understanding Collapse](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/understanding-collapse/introducing-collapse/58BEF759BB7D095A3AE6B1C2B5E4E1B9) distinguishes political fragmentation from disappearance of a people. Conquests, failed capitals and secessions retain cultures, settlements, religion and linked historical records.
- [Nature: missing-data critique of religion/complexity timing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03655-4) illustrates contested causal claims. Religion is not a mandatory moralizing-god upgrade or an inherent military bonus. Local traditions, patronage, neighboring transmission and reform are separate from political ownership.
- [Cambridge: medieval agricultural techniques](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-economic-history-of-europe-from-the-decline-of-the-roman-empire/evolution-of-agricultural-technique/D9F8696FE71977F5F07EED5F9B7AF705) and [Oxford: technology diffusion](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195074772.003.0003) inform gradual, contact-assisted adoption and links between agricultural productivity, transport and craft practices.

These sources support mechanisms and counterexamples, **not our numerical coefficients**. All thresholds/probabilities are explicit game priors. No source code was copied.

## State and progression

The following baseline description concerns the initial history implementation; later dated sections above supersede it. In particular, history13 has named crop lineages and37capabilities rather than the original15-practice ceiling, and calendar2 uses sampled actor timing rather than a universal50-year cultural tick.

`PrehistoryState.History` is optional and independently hashed only in prehistory-6. Prehistory-1 through 5 keep their earlier route, progression and hash contracts. Loading never silently upgrades a history. Start a new simulation for the new model; existing geography can be reused in a fresh world.

Historical state distinguishes:

- Regional cultural identities, inherited language-family labels, resources, subsistence, sedentism, surplus, institutions and adopted practices.
- Individual settlement cells and local, disjoint supply catchments; urban residents are separate from rural patch populations. Founders and migration are transfers, not population creation.
- Political owners, capitals, rulers/houses, parent/successor states, founding/end dates, treasury, army levy, cohesion, legitimacy and exhaustion.
- Dominant local religious tradition, holy place, parent tradition, ritual, ethos and reform history. This is not a full minority-faith census.
- Pairwise contact, trade, alliances, wars and truces. Trade buffers food and enables exchange; it is not a commodity-market simulation.
- Lifetime linked events alongside the short 256-event UI feed. Campaign export includes peoples, current/fallen states and the complete dated event archive.

Regional cultural emergence requires a population-supported concentration of storable resources. Sparse, cold, arid or mobile communities can persist without becoming storage-centered regional networks. They still have human culture: the existing UI label “advanced culture” denotes a modeled settlement/exchange institution, **not cultural or moral worth**.

New states start at an established settlement, with sustained urban population, food reserves, local surplus and institutions. Political claims subsequently spread one frontier per decade within an accessible market catchment. Town supply is allocated by a deterministic, terrain-costed multi-source graph search; no cell supplies several cities. Roads and accounting practices increase effective reach. At very coarse resolution a single claimed cell remains a large visible area; subcell political districts are not simulated.

Wars require contact and a reachable target. Terrain, fortification, equipment, cohesion, allies and seeded uncertainty affect results. Campaign casualties reduce resident population; conquest changes political ownership, not cultural identity. Expansion is restrained by cost, exhaustion and cohesion. Distant peripheral towns can secede; states can lose their capitals or disappear while their peoples survive. Multi-city, multi-cultural states are classified as tributary empires. Armies are abstract levies included in the population, not separately counted inhabitants or animated units.

Annual harvests, reserves, shortfalls, urban growth, migration and epidemic mortality continue. Trade contacts increase epidemic exposure; recovery creates a temporary immunity window. This is a simple town-level outbreak model, not compartmental epidemiology or a universal repeated plague applied to all humans.

## Technologies and limits

Fifteen branching practices stop at a pre-gunpowder medieval ceiling: storage, cultivation, pottery, sailing, irrigation, copper, writing/accounts, bronze, roads, iron, coinage, mills, steel, navigation and scholastic learning. No gunpowder, industrial or modern technology exists in the catalog. Research requires prerequisites and local opportunities; contacts accelerate adoption. Yield, transport, taxation, defenses, warfare and sea dispersal consume the relevant practices. There is no universal Bronze Age → Iron Age → civilization requirement.

Ore deposits, crop cultivars and domestic-animal breeding are not explicitly simulated; metals and domestication use broad opportunity/knowledge proxies. Religion contains generated practices and lineage, not a comprehensive theology. Language labels record family identity but phonetic evolution is not modeled. Leaders have named successions, not a complete individual genealogy. Campaign lore records what actually occurred; detailed biographies, myths and literary prose remain future narrative layers.
