# History32: funded replacement port suppliers (W candidate)

This is a separately opt-in extension of the V import-dependent harbor model, not a new generator or a grant of crops, settlers, or territorial control. Promotion requires the focused tests and matched continuations described below; the existence of this document is not a claim of a completed realism pass.

## Motivation and evidence

In actual V final ledgers, Pangeae31415 had 34 provisioned ports, 27 surviving, with 66,050 current residents; six of seven abandoned ports also had an abandoned original source. IceAge27182 had 126 ports, 34 surviving, with 56,746 residents; 54 of 92 abandoned ports had a dead original source. These final co-occurrences do not establish the cause of abandonment. They do expose a model restriction worth testing: every import-dependent port was tied permanently to one founding warehouse, even when another real supported community could supply it.

The official [Ostia archaeological park account](https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/en/trails/grain-and-bread/) describes multiple supplying provinces, actual maritime and river transport, warehouses, and administrative work. Allowing a funded replacement source is a model inference from that evidence, not empirical calibration of the numbers below and not a claim that all ancient port populations were import-dependent.

## Compatibility and explicit activation

`HistoricalProvisionedPorts.SupplierRouting` is nullable. Null retains V behavior exactly, including future ports founded while continuing that old collection. `HistoricalProvisionedPort.SupplierHistory` is also nullable; both null additions are omitted from canonical hashing.

Freshly initialized port collections leave routing **disabled**: the first matched Earth holdout regressed, so this experiment is not a default rule. `UpgradeProvisionedPortSuppliers` is the explicit experimental copy-and-seal upgrade for an existing history32 state. It does not change existing food, people, money, control, crops, or source choices. It attributes the unchanged V lifetime cargo to its actual original supplier and preserves the separate activation date. Reads and atlas queries never upgrade a saved world.

## Bounded actor work and actual costs

Only the existing port city event considers replacement, and only when its current supplier cannot cover current rations from actual stock. A deterministic portfolio contains at most four actual indexed towns of the current owner. It does not enumerate countries, empire cells, regions, coastline cells, or every town.

Candidates must be supported, non-abandoned, non-provisioned towns of that same current owner, with actual rural residents and rural production capacity. A genuinely producing younger hinterland farm may replace an older founding depot; town IDs are not a reason to reject real local food. Excluding all dependent-port replacement sources prevents port supply cycles. Legacy original sources retain their older-ID rule and existing import-dependent chains. Foreign trade contracts remain out of scope; neither independence nor shared culture creates free supplies.

The current live controlled maritime office, actual departure coast, sea lane, technical range, institutional route, and ordinary logistics preflight must remain usable. An inland source still needs a real serviced coastal staging point. A new source needs exportable warehouse food, real setup money and recurring freight capacity. The cheapest delivered-food option uses deterministic town-ID ties. Successful replacement pays route setup, then the ordinary cargo transaction debits the actual warehouse and treasury. Every ration reconciles sent = received + lost; no farmer household, harvest, population, or new field appears.

The same-year city-event guard remains authoritative. A failed search or failed payment does not change source, debit setup, or rescue the port from ordinary decline. The existing bounded eight-site logistics preflight may validate a preferred source, but a fallback town different from that exact candidate is never accepted as an implicit fifth source.

## Dated attribution without an unbounded actor ledger

Each enabled port preserves the original supplier and activation year, plus at most 16 recent dated supplier-account segments. Each segment records supplier, departure, start/end, sent/received/lost cargo, freight, and setup payments. Completed older segments roll into reconciled earlier totals; the original supplier remains explicit. The ordinary historical archive retains every dated supplier-change event, rather than forcing routine city events to scan centuries of prior suppliers.

The current segment must agree with the port's actual source/departure. Segment plus rolled totals must equal lifetime cargo/freight. An atlas must distinguish lifetime totals from the current supplier's segment; it must not attribute every historical delivery to the latest source.

## Validation and limitations

Focused validation covers old-null copy/hash/read/continuation, explicit activation, 2,000 rolled accounting segments, fixed bounds, actual donor-stock loss with a genuinely eligible replacement, unavailable funds/food/foreign/self/dependent sources, no fictitious fields/people, repeated dates, save/load, and publication partitioning. Actual matching V/W continuations must additionally check whether ports survive more plausibly, without an unearned town boom or a change to pristine state-origin gates.

This remains a coarse warehouse-and-route model. It does not yet support multiple simultaneous suppliers, market prices, foreign food contracts, insurance, or automatic conversion to locally supported agriculture. The V maximum handling capacity remains unchanged. Those are distinct extensions, not reasons to manufacture local food or bypass political permission here.

### First matched outcomes: not accepted

Two supplier-only 500-year continuations used identical V inputs, with all other new optional rules left disabled. These are conditional continuation experiments, not fresh-world generation comparisons. Artifact reports are `D:/WorldGenBuildArtifacts/history32-w-suppliers-{iceage,earth}500/comparison.json`.

| Actual V input | Original-port survivors, unchanged V / routing | All live ports, V / routing | Whole population, V / routing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| IceAge27182, year457100 | 19 / 20 | 31 / 34 | 1.153 billion / 1.128 billion |
| Earth73421, year129700 | 63 / 28 | 116 / 63 | 729.9 million / 671.1 million |

IceAge made 90 paid supplier changes across16 ports; Earth made246 across37. Lifetime food, loss, freight and setup accounts validate, but conservation is not a realism acceptance test. The Earth holdout is materially worse. **Do not promote this initial replacement policy on the strength of the small IceAge gain.**

The current candidate reacts to a single consumption shortfall, even when the original warehouse can still provide partial food. There is no persistence/cooldown condition, so repeated paid switches and exposure of additional source farms to export pressure are plausible failure mechanisms. Also, the shared source field governs both food shipping and the separate urban-colonist expansion cohort; changing food supplier can unintentionally change the pool of future colonists. Those are hypotheses to test with actual actor traces, not conclusions established by final totals. The exact old-null V branch still reproduces its frozen one-year continuation hash.

An optional engine-only `SupplierRoutingObserver` receives immutable primitive diagnostics when a supplier changes. It is not saved or hashed, and the null path allocates no trace. Records distinguish source death, changed ownership, loss of support, small source, empty stock, partial food and remaining route/fiscal failures. New source cargo values describe the validated shipment plan; the saved segment ledger remains the authoritative actually paid/delivered account. Observer-on/off full-state hash equality is a required regression check. No production correction should be selected merely to force these two final totals upward.
