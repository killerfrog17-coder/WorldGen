# History 32: maritime networks and bounded local settlement

This describes the optional `history-32` rules, not a retroactive change to
history-31 saves. It is a game-scale model, not a reconstructed economic or
military history of Earth. The geographical mechanisms operate on any map;
there are no Roman, Mongol, European, Antarctic or Fertile Crescent masks.

## Causal changes

An administrative office is not a settlement. Offices record funded services
and control; they do not create 250 people every time a flag is planted. A real
sponsored overseas port separately recruits a small urban and agricultural
cohort from an actual source town's urban, suburban, rural and state-remote
household accounts. The transfer conserves world population. Startup food,
voyage provisions and treasury investment are separate charges. A successful
office can be retried later; inability to settle immediately is not permanent.

The migrants retain their actual source town's culture and available crops,
including when that town is not the state's ruling culture. Their crop
repertoire must support the destination. Nearby native regions are not simply
converted or taught a package because a metropolitan culture exists elsewhere.
The target town cell records its founders; contact with native residents is
handled separately by the existing physical exchange rules.

The expedition planner checks at most four actual departure nodes and six
known chart targets per departure. It checks real provisioning before choosing
a destination. A supported inland town may provision its actual serviced,
owned, same-landmass harbor within its administrative service radius; being
close to a painted coastline is insufficient. Long unsupported voyages require
deployed navigation and written administration. Money alone is not food.

Native towns can negotiate protection or incorporation, but a rich stranger
does not automatically buy the planet. Coercive expeditions require an actual
supplied campaign; nuclear deterrence can prevent that campaign. Their events
must not narrate a war which the campaign system refused to create.

## Local networks rather than a worldwide cultural lottery

The former secondary-town opportunity sampled one parent from a culture's
entire active town portfolio. A port on another continent could therefore be
starved by hundreds of homeland cities. New history-32 town actors each have
their own bounded opportunity on their existing scheduled city process. The
rate depends on actual supported population, transferable households, local
capacity pressure, residence, civic practice and deployed transport. Only a
rare successful opportunity samples up to three local paths. Their hop count
uses cell area to propose approximately 120–240 km reach, capped at eight
neighbor steps; actual physical delivery still limits the result. It does not
inspect all cells or all towns of a culture/state.

An actual move still requires viable source crops at the destination, crowding
and committed-land limits, physical institutional routing, real food and (for
states) real construction finances. Funded local communities retain the
funding polity rather than reverting to their culture's obsolete colonial
sponsor. The original first-town opportunity is unchanged. Stable prestate
centers require at least 1,000 urban residents, 1,600 transferable households
and 80 years' residence; supported civic centers need 30 years. Base annual
opportunities are .0015 and .003 respectively, before bounded size, pressure,
transport and actual destination constraints. These are calibration priors,
not empirical estimates of all Neolithic settlement formation.

History-32 neighborhood links use the shared institutional transport predicate,
whose land reach is 600 transport-adjusted effective kilometers. The former
360-unit neighborhood rule still applies unchanged to history-31 saves. This
is a deliberate reach harmonization, not a claim that the two rules had
identical thresholds. Rainforest/desert/relief and latitude costs still divide
physical range; the 280-km local-neighbor query, relief/roads constraints and
actual sea-lane/sailing conditions remain. On coarse maps, four-to-six fixed
hops had proposed 367–900 km destinations while missing viable 125–173 km
neighbors, even when actual crops and founding households were adequate.

Charts and administrative office budgets grow with deployed capabilities and
urban administrative capacity, with fixed finite ceilings. Neither a large
budget nor combustion engines alone grants sovereignty over uninhabitable
icecaps. Real inhabited cold regions and useful supported territories remain
possible. Scientific polar stations are not simulated by this change.

## Military geography

Mounted effects require an actually adopted animal with a riding-suitability
trait, distinct from traction or general domesticability. Open canopy, pasture,
waterlogging, flooding, relief and permafrost affect effectiveness; savanna is
not treated as closed rainforest merely because it includes a forest habitat
flag. Camel browse modestly eases horse-like dry-pasture constraints. Siege and
engineering practice and actual supplied maritime access affect campaign
theaters. These are bounded tactical/supply multipliers, not unit-by-unit
battles or fully simulated garrisons.

Primary historical motivation: the [ORBIS methods paper](https://orbis.stanford.edu/orbis2012/ORBIS_v1paper_20120501.pdf)
models mode-, season- and network-dependent Roman transport costs. A
[Mongol withdrawal study](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep25606) proposes
wetness, pasture and fortified geography among the relevant constraints; its
interpretation is not a universal single-cause theorem. Neither source is a
license to give all coastal states free Mediterranean supply or every open
plain a Mongol identity.

## Evidence and limits

The integration fixtures exercise actual source-town household accounts,
negative food/treasury cases, crop constraints, funded ownership, physical
routes, no duplicate founding, save integrity and unchanged loaded inputs.
The capped planner, chart budgets and military effects have separate tests.
Controlled fixtures are labeled and do not count as naturally arising ports.

Actual shared-renderer screenshots were independently viewed for early and
late eras, not just summary logs. Round G Continents Plus at 200,000 cells
showed populated coastal networks on all major landmasses by early modern,
48 surviving states and roughly 587 live towns after 900 postmodern years.
It was visibly much better than earlier office-only coastal paint. This is one
control, not proof of universal historical plausibility. Round H Earth (the
earlier `earth-native-2` comparison, not the new 203-entry native-3 pack) still
left India and China too weakly connected politically despite growing Atlantic
and other overseas ports. Those failures motivated local settlement work;
they must not be hidden by citing only the stronger generated-world result.
An additional archipelago seed had no state by 420,000 years and remains a
hard ecological control, rather than being discarded from the review.

Round J Archipelago 42 exposed a rejected calibration: Neolithic at 182,000,
first state only at 630,619, with roughly 20,000 abandoned towns. A saved
450,000-year diagnostic showed only three live centers (250–347 urban people)
and zero eligible local parents despite adequate transferable rural households.
Their actual worker-supported capacities were 266–408 urban residents; most
villages dispersed before their farms could grow enough to support civic
concentration. Raising a local opportunity alone cannot fix an urban threshold
which no surviving parent reaches. The independent origin ecology/resource/
homeland requirements are not removed to hide this failure. Subsequent
productive-village survival and local-network calibration requires a fresh
comparison, including this failed seed.

The [original European Neolithic demographic study](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1398968/1/ncomms3486.pdf)
finds regional population surges and declines rather than continuous growth;
its tested climate proxies generally do not explain that pattern. This
motivates maintaining endogenous disruption and population pressure alongside
climate, not making all settlement turnover a weather timer. It does not
calibrate this model's particular village survival coefficients or imply that
all productive farming networks inevitably form states.

The renderer screenshots do not establish native Unity UI correctness. The
parent review records player benchmarks and UI checks separately. Current
coarse population identities, state-remote recruitment and immediate deployed
specialist availability are aggregates, not literal household genealogies or
shipping manifests. There is no continuous inventory simulation of rations,
ore or port garrisons. Modern large-scale annexation/globalization and the
balance between local diversity and naval hegemonies still require multiple
seeds and direct map inspection after each material change. Explicit ecology
profiles must be recorded: the review command historically defaults to native-2,
so an omitted optional profile argument is not evidence about native-3.
