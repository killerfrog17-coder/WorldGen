## Candidate B: conserved military operations

Fresh realm revision 3 adds paid, trained manpower stocks and concurrent-front
reservations. Casualties reduce both the field stock and exactly one existing
population cohort. Epoch changes invalidate old reservations in O(1); no army
is reset from ideal population each state turn or multiplied by a policy budget.
Food, wages, transport, terrain, marching time and siege work constrain real
field forces. A nominal 0.01-power force cannot impose whole-country submission
before fighting. Settlements distinguish frontier, tribute, route and conquest
objectives; credible core pressure is required for political submission.

This remains an aggregate field-campaign model, not simulated tactical units.
Annual supplied engagements and proxy fortification capacity are explicit
approximations. Physical advance remains area-based, not one terrain cell per
turn. Historical upgrade is explicit; realm 1/2 continuations retain their rules.

## Follow-up causes under investigation

1. Bounded retirement/reassignment of unsupported territorial service, keeping
   recognized sovereignty distinct from actual administration.
2. Returning imperial fleets need institutions, stores, repair workshops and
   trained administrative practice, not only ocean wayfinding inherited from
   oral seafarers. Independent household/island settlement must remain possible.
3. Religion needs live carriers, inherited legal/endowment continuity and
   enduring place-bound forms, rather than every supported local rite becoming
   a portable or missionary stage. Identical generic theology is not adequate.
4. Origins, city survival, inland diffusion and the technical bottleneck need
   causal opportunity records, not merely age labels or a global speed multiplier.

Historical comparison sources (coefficients remain game priors):

- [Met: first cities](https://www.metmuseum.org/it/essays/art-of-the-first-cities-in-the-third-millennium-b-c)

  discusses widespread knowledge, competition, trade, conquest and colonization;
  inhabited lands are not empty targets awaiting colonists.
