## Historical interpretation and limitations

The [Oxford History of Science Museum's instrument-making evidence](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/almizan/ScienceAndCraft.html)
connects mathematical texts with trained workshop practice and collaboration.
It supports retaining actual practice and learned transmission rather than a
calendar unlock. It does not calibrate85/70/60 or a universal exponential rank.

There is also a separate graph limitation. The current variable is named
"Scholastic learning" and requires writing3, water/wind mills2 and coinage2.
It should not be read as the universal beginning of organized mathematical
thought: [Columbia's Plimpton322 artifact](https://exhibitions.library.columbia.edu/exhibits/show/plimpton/mathematics/page-2)
dates to approximately1820–1762BCE and is interpreted as possible mathematical
teaching material. Nor is all printing the culmination of one Western school
sequence: the [British Library's Dunhuang evidence](https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/dunhuang/collection-items/cave-17-the-library-cave/)
documents a donor-sponsored printed Buddhist text in868CE. These primary
collection examples motivate a future review of what the abstract ranks mean;
this narrow cost patch deliberately does NOT silently rewrite that graph.
