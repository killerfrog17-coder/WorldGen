# History32: provisioned overseas harbors

This is an experimental history32 extension, not a new generation route or a claim to model maritime economies in full. Old history31 rules and old history32 saves with a null extension retain their existing behavior. Fresh history32 initializes the empty sparse ledger; older candidate32 saves require the explicit `UpgradeProvisionedPorts` operation.

## Historical motivation and actual diagnosis

Not every overseas town begins as an exported farming village. Singapore's National Library reproduces Raffles' contemporary account of existing inhabitants, local vegetables and boat-building, and rice and salt obtainable through Siamese trade. The account and the 1819 agreements describe a negotiated trading settlement, not an empty island where a European crop package instantly became productive. [NLB, contemporary correspondence and treaty context](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/chapters-of-asia/chapters-on-asia-2014-2017-teochew-1819-founding-singapore/).

Jamestown illustrates the converse: initial arrivals, resupply and indigenous food exchanges did not guarantee food security. Drought, conflict and interrupted supplies contributed to severe mortality and an attempted abandonment. This supports explicit dependent supply and possible failure, not a coast-wide virtual food allowance. [National Park Service, Jamestown](https://www.nps.gov/jame/learn/historyculture/a-short-history-of-jamestown.htm).

The saved S2 Earth audit found an actual funded route from supplier town 499 to Sri Lankan cell 115037. The supplier had only its actual wheat/olive repertoire, with local field yield about 0.0155; neither the target nor its bounded nearby field probes supported those carried crops. Country identity or a radio connection must not invent rice. That target was a known chart, **not yet an owned funded harbor office**; its ruler's cohesion 524 was below the existing 550 charter requirement. The alternative below still requires the real office and does not waive those political/route conditions.

`CommunityUrbanImports` distributes analytic national remote-farm capacity, not debited warehouse cargo. `LivingUrbanImportLimit` is a handling ceiling, not an actual food source. Neither is used to create food for these ports. The old annual-food routine's all-town search remains unused.

## Sparse, paid supply basis

`HistoricalColonialConnections.ProvisionedPorts` is nullable. Its immutable `ByTown` index identifies sparse `HistoricalProvisionedPort` records; ordinary farmer towns have no record and follow their existing path.

A new provisioned harbor requires all of the following:

- An existing owned, live maritime office, an actual supported supplying town, a serviced departure, physically allowed route, deployed navigation/administration, funded public works and current territorial interest.
- A habitable non-permanent-ice coast, potable-water opportunity, free local site and the normal bounded spatial/land-competition limits. A claim alone is not a food source.
- Unsuitable **carried** fields. If that source can already found a farming town there, its normal farm-settler path remains in use.
- 100–250 real urban emigrants: at most one twentieth of the supplier's urban population above 1,500. No remote national population or hypothetical farming households are recruited for this basis.
- Actual warehouse stocks for three years of initial migrant rations plus voyage food, a source safety reserve, construction money and affordable recurring freight.

The common founding transaction is revalidated at commit. It reserves a 10 km² harbor footprint, zero rural capacity and zero cultivated fields, transfers urban people and initial stocks exactly once, consumes voyage provisions and pays investment exactly once. It does not create another independent state.

## Recurring food, failure and growth

The existing city event handles one fixed supplier, not a search of states, coasts or cities. It rechecks the actual owner, office/service route, supplier survival, supported warehouses and funded public works. A finite cargo is debited from warehouse surplus above half the source urban population; sent cargo equals received food plus explicit transport loss. Shipping consumes treasury funds.

The authored maritime-retention index uses deployed sailing, navigation, ocean navigation, steam, engines, storage and pottery. Land transport improves only the actual inland staging leg, not ocean shipping. These coefficients are comparative game priors, not estimated historical shipping-loss rates. At most three years of current-stock opportunity are credited during a stale city review; the full elapsed interval still consumes food. Current resources cannot prove centuries of uninterrupted provision.

The recipient consumes received food **once**. There is no parallel local harvest or virtual national-import credit. Its installed handling capacity is a population ceiling, not a promise that food exists. Normal mortality, disease, insecurity, site disruption, shortages, decline and abandonment remain. Surviving households disperse through the existing abandonment ledger; the vacant office re-enters the bounded settlement-retry queue. Loss of a supplier, route, office or money is not silently repaired by a global substitute.

An active healthy harbor can attempt one paid handling expansion after 40 years, on the same city actor. It requires an actual recent delivery, current source households/stocks, physical route, construction funds and recurring freight coverage. Each investment adds at most 25% installed capacity and **actual** source urban migrants, with their stocks and voyage rations charged once. The deployment/public-works ceiling ranges from 250 to 10,250 residents. There are no free remote arrivals, invented suburban farmers, new fields or crop unlocks. This allows genuinely provisioned towns to grow beyond markers while keeping their supply burden explicit.

The record exposes founding investment and cargo, installed residents, actual last delivery interval/date, cumulative sent/received/lost food, shipping cost, paid expansions and additional migrants. A receipt over several years must not be labeled as an annual rate without division by its recorded interval.

## Deliberate limitations

- One named warehouse supplier; no automatic replacement, regional wild-fish harvest, unaccounted indigenous food or global ruling-culture pantry.
- Native named towns could eventually provide negotiated surplus through actual bilateral trade, but this version requires the same current government. Static marine ecology is not a renewable food inventory and is not treated as one.
- Abstract cargo/food/account units, no literal ship manifests, berth geometry, convoy interception, household age structure or commodity-specific spoilage.
- No automatic conversion into a farming town. Imported residents do not acquire locally viable crops by receiving unrelated technologies.
- Migrants use the destination town's coarse culture account and ancestry-mixing proxy; individuals and multiple permanent urban cultural populations are not represented.
- Actual modern-global coverage must be assessed by full Earth/generated-map continuations and screenshots; unit transactions do not prove realistic colonization by themselves.

## Focused evidence

The unchanged S2 Earth snapshot supplied a genuine positive: state 103, town 499, existing office 208 at cell 71720. 234 urban residents moved, no rural/remote people were invented, and only 10 km² was reserved. A recurring cargo of 500 yielded 426 received and 74 lost, with 200 fiscal account units charged. After actual recent cargo and funded expansion, installed handling rose from 250 to 313 with 63 additional real urban migrants.

Focused tests cover null canonical compatibility, explicit upgrade/copy, startup and recurring food/fiscal/population conservation, commit-time resource revalidation, same-date duplicate protection, actual city consumption without duplicate harvest, paid expansion and its negatives, urban-only multi-year upkeep, source cutoff leading to abandonment, pending-office lifecycle, partitioning, real snapshot serialization and continuation.

Actual state/technology PNGs were rendered and viewed, not inferred from the counts:

- Earth Bronze year 121900: one compact central-North-American origin, not simultaneous distant state creation. Postmodern +900 year 129600: genuine towns now occur around Europe, Mediterranean, Japan/Korea, Australia and African coasts, but urban concentration remains extremely North-American. This is **not** accepted as a realistic global-modern Earth result.
- Continents Plus Bronze year 164600: two geographically separate developing centers. Early Modern year 170200: a dense southern urban belt and eastern continental polities, with scattered overseas beginnings rather than instant world paint. Postmodern +900 year 171700: populated coastal networks on every major landmass and differentiated regional states, with cold/mountain/desert interiors less occupied. This is visibly more globally connected, not proof that every border or growth rate is correct.
- The saved Earth's abiotic errors still make much of India implausibly arid. The supplied-port mechanism must not disguise that by granting wheat/rice fields against the actual saved terrain/climate inputs. Fixed-supplier dependence and absence of a real local-agriculture transition remain separate possible limits on development after a foothold exists; they require causal inspection, not a blanket resource bypass.
